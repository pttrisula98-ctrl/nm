Editorial Policy

Last updated: November 18, 2026

This policy describes how Money Mechanics chooses topics, researches claims, writes guides, corrects mistakes, and keeps content current. It applies to every article on this site.

Choosing topics

We pick topics based on questions that real readers ask about money decisions: how an account works, what a rule means, or how to compare two options. We do not write about individual stocks, cryptocurrencies, or products in order to recommend them.

Research and sourcing

Factual claims about taxes, retirement accounts, deposit insurance, securities, and government benefits are checked against primary sources. These include IRS publications, SEC and Investor.gov materials, FDIC and NCUA guidance, Federal Reserve data, Social Security Administration rules, and official plan or product documents. Secondary commentary may add context but does not replace a primary source.

Writing standards

Each guide explains the mechanics first, uses worked examples with clearly stated assumptions, and separates established rules from matters of judgment. When experts or the evidence disagree, we say so. We avoid urgent language, guaranteed-return claims, and headlines designed to provoke fear.

Fact-checking

Before publication, a second editor checks each figure, date, threshold, and citation against its source. Numbers such as contribution limits, tax brackets, and insurance caps are verified against the most recent official publication available at the time of writing.

Updates and corrections

Financial rules change. Every guide shows its publication and last-updated dates. When a rule, rate, or limit changes, we revise the affected guide and record the change. If you find an error, please send us the details through our contact page. We correct confirmed errors promptly and note significant corrections on the page.

Independence and disclosures

We do not accept payment for editorial placement and do not take commissions from the products we discuss. Advertising is shown on this site, including Google AdSense units, but it is kept separate from editorial content. We will disclose any relationship that could reasonably be seen as affecting our analysis.

Limits of our content

Our guides are educational. They cannot account for your tax situation, debts, goals, or local rules. For decisions with large consequences, consult a qualified professional.
