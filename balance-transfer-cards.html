Balance Transfer Cards: When the Fee Beats the Interest

By Money Mechanics Editorial Team · Published November 8, 2026 · Updated November 8, 2026 · 6 min read

A balance transfer card lets you move existing credit card debt to a new card, often with a low or 0% introductory rate for a limited time. The appeal is clear, but the card usually charges a transfer fee, and the promotional rate ends. Whether the move saves money depends on the fee, how long you need to repay, and whether you can avoid new debt.

How the offer usually works

You apply for a card that offers a promotional rate on transferred balances. If approved, you request the transfer, and the issuer pays off the old balance. You then owe the new card issuer at the promotional rate for a set period, after which the standard purchase rate applies. Transfers typically carry a fee, commonly a percentage of the amount moved, with a minimum charge. Purchases may be charged at a different, often higher, rate than transfers, so check the terms.

Running the numbers

The key comparison is between the transfer fee and the interest you would otherwise pay during the same period. Suppose you have $5,000 on a card at 24% interest, and you can move it to a card with a 3% transfer fee and a 0% introductory rate for 15 months.

ItemAmount
Transfer fee (3% of $5,000)$150
Interest on old card for 12 months at 24% (approximate, if balance stays flat)about $1,200
Interest on new card during promo period$0
Estimated savings over 12 months, before any other costsabout $1,050

These figures are approximate and assume no new purchases and no payments. In practice, your balance will fall as you pay, which reduces the interest you would have paid on the old card. Redo the calculation with the actual repayment schedule you can sustain.

The break-even test: Compare the fee with the interest you would pay at the old rate over the months you expect to need. If the fee is larger than that interest, the transfer does not help.

How long is the promotional period?

The promotional period determines how much time you have to repay without interest. A longer period reduces the monthly payment needed, but it does not change the total you must repay. Work backward from the promotional end date: divide the balance by the number of months, and check whether that monthly amount fits your budget. If it does not, the promotion may not give you enough time, and you could be left with a high rate on the remaining balance.

Credit score effects

Applying for a new card typically triggers a hard inquiry, which can cause a small, temporary dip in your score. Opening a new account also lowers your average account age. However, moving a balance from one card to another does not change your total debt, and it can lower your utilization on the old card. Many people see a net improvement if they keep the old card open and avoid new balances. Closing the old card after transferring may reduce your available credit and raise utilization, so consider keeping it open.

The one rule never to break

Do not use the new card for new purchases while you are paying off the transferred balance. Many cards apply payments to the lowest-rate balance first, and purchases may accrue interest at a higher rate immediately. Adding new debt to a balance transfer card is one of the most common reasons these plans fail. If you are tempted to spend on the card, consider keeping it closed to new purchases in your head, or using a different card for everyday spending.

When a transfer is a poor idea

A balance transfer may not be worth it if the fee is high relative to the interest you would save, if you cannot repay before the promotion ends, if the credit limit is too low to cover the full balance, or if you are likely to keep spending on your cards. In those cases, a repayment plan such as the debt avalanche may be more effective.

Common mistakes

  • Ignoring the transfer fee when comparing offers.
  • Missing a payment, which can cancel the promotional rate.
  • Making new purchases on the transfer card.
  • Assuming the promotional rate applies to purchases as well as transfers.
  • Transferring a balance without a clear plan to repay before the promo ends.

Frequently asked questions

Can I transfer a balance between cards from the same issuer?

Many issuers do not allow transfers between their own cards. Check the terms before applying.

What if I miss a payment?

Missing a payment can lead to penalties and may end the promotional rate. Set up automatic payments of at least the minimum due.

Does the transfer appear immediately?

Transfers can take several days to weeks to complete. Keep paying the old card until the transfer posts, to avoid late payments.
