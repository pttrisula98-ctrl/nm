Subscription Audit: Find and Cancel What You Don't Use

By Money Mechanics Editorial Team · Published October 27, 2026 · Updated October 27, 2026 · 5 min read

Recurring charges are easy to ignore because each one looks small. A streaming service here, a cloud storage plan there, and an app you tried once all add up to a monthly cost that most people underestimate. A subscription audit takes about half an hour and usually finds money you can redirect to goals that matter more to you.

Step 1: Gather three months of statements

Download statements from every bank account, credit card, and digital wallet you use. Three months is the minimum, because some subscriptions bill quarterly or annually and will not appear in a single month. If you have a card you rarely use, include it, since automatic renewals often run on cards that are otherwise dormant.

Step 2: List every recurring charge

Scan each statement for charges that repeat on a regular schedule. Include streaming services, music, news, software, cloud storage, gaming, fitness apps, meal kits, delivery memberships, online courses, and digital tools. Also look for annual charges, which are easy to forget until they renew. Write each one down with four fields: the merchant name, the amount, the billing frequency, and the next renewal date if the statement shows it.

Charges can use unfamiliar names, especially when a merchant uses a parent company or payment processor. If you do not recognize a charge, search the merchant name in your email for a receipt, or check the company's website.

Step 3: Evaluate each subscription

For each item, ask four questions. When did I last use it? What would I do if it were gone? Is there a cheaper plan or a free alternative? Would I choose to pay for it again today? Be honest with yourself. A subscription you use daily and value deserves a place in your budget. One you have not opened in two months probably does not.

The example costs are illustrative. The decisions show the thinking, not a fixed verdict for any service.

Step 4: Cancel, downgrade, or keep

Once you have decided, act on it immediately. Cancelling in the same session prevents the common trap of intending to cancel and forgetting. Many services offer cancellation in account settings, while others require contacting customer support. Keep a screenshot or confirmation email as proof. For annual plans, check whether you can still get a refund or whether the service continues until the end of the billing period.

Set a reminder: Many free trials convert to paid plans automatically. When you sign up for a trial, add a calendar reminder a few days before it ends.

Step 5: Prevent subscription creep

Once your audit is complete, set up a simple habit to keep subscriptions in check. Review your recurring charges every three or four months. Keep a short list of current subscriptions in your budget or spreadsheet, and add new ones to it before you start paying. Some people find it helpful to set a rule, such as needing to cancel one service before adding another.

Common mistakes

  • Looking only at the most recent statement and missing charges that bill annually.
  • Keeping a service "just in case" for months without using it.
  • Switching to a cheaper plan that you then fail to use, which wastes the savings.
  • Forgetting that a subscription paid through a family plan or app store may renew from a different account.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I do a subscription audit?

Every three to six months is a good rhythm. Also review after you start a new job, move, or change your phone or computer, because these often bring new subscriptions.

Should I cancel a subscription I rarely use but really value?

Often you can pause it, downgrade it, or switch to an annual plan if it is cheaper overall. If you rarely use something but would miss it, check whether it is worth the monthly cost.

Can I find subscriptions through my bank?

Some banks and budgeting tools flag recurring charges automatically. These are helpful starting points, but they can miss some merchants, so check your statements directly.
